Observe

1. One line of mix costs 31 write() system calls: 30 letters and the
   new line. Between two of these calls the other process, running on
   another CPU, can write its own letter. So the letters mix.

2. sem_down(3); before the inner for, and sem_up(3); after it. Everything
   between them, the 31 writes of one line, is the critical section.

3. wakeup(s) wakes every process that sleeps on s, but sem_up added only
   one unit. The first to get the spinlock takes it; the others find
   value 0 again and must go back to sleep. A process that never slept
   can also take the unit first.

4. sem_up needs the same spinlock, so it would spin forever and nobody
   could wake the sleeper. This kernel stops even earlier: I removed the
   release() line in a separate copy of the tree, and semtest printed
   "panic: sched locks". The check is in sched(), kernel/proc.c: a
   process may hold only its own p->lock when it gives up the CPU.
